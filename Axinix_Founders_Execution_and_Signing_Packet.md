## 2. BINDING COVENANTS & GOVERNANCE MECHANICS

### 2.1 Ratification of the Corporate Charter
All Partners explicitly affirm, adopt, and incorporate by reference the **Axinix Corporate Charter & Commercial Playbook** as the binding constitutional and operational policy of Axinix Technologies.

### 2.2 Fiduciary Declaration of Trust (By Ajay)
> *"I, **Ajay**, solemnly affirm and declare that I hold the commercial trade name **Axinix Technologies**, all current and future business bank accounts, client contracts, software licenses, intellectual property licenses, and operational assets associated therewith, **IN TRUST** for the collective, equal benefit of the eight founding participants. I disclaim any sole proprietary beneficial interest in the net distributable profits or institutional assets of Axinix Technologies beyond my individual entitlement under the Corporate Charter."*

### 2.3 Mutual Partner Indemnity (By the 7 Co-Founders)
All seven co-founding partners (**Arunbharathi, Boopalan, Guru Prasanth, Karuppasamy, Rithika, Ruthran, and Siva**) jointly, severally, and irrevocably agree to indemnify, protect, and hold harmless Ajay against all bona fide commercial losses, tax assessments (GST/Income Tax), vendor claims, and regulatory liabilities incurred in the ordinary and approved course of Axinix Technologies' operations.

### 2.4 The Two-Tier Profit Distribution Engine
Distributable Net Profit shall be computed monthly and divided into two equal pools:
* **Fixed Base Pool (50%):** Arunbharathi receives 15%; each of the seven co-founders receives 5%. This fixed base allocation is protected against temporary periods of illness or reduced activity and cannot be altered without a 100% unanimous vote.
* **Monthly Performance Pool (50%):** Allocated based on peer-audited deliverables, code quality, and commercial impact in accordance with Schedule B of the Corporate Charter ($\sum \text{Score} = 100\%$).

### 2.5 Strict 100% Unanimity on Reserved Critical Matters
Under no circumstances may a majority vote override any of the following nine reserved matters:
1. Alteration of capital or economic ownership allocations.
2. Amendment of the profit-sharing framework.
3. Admission or removal of a founding partner.
4. Borrowing money, bank loans, or incurring major financial debt.
5. Selling, encumbering, or transferring company assets or IP.
6. Entering major strategic partnerships or joint ventures.
7. Changing the fundamental business direction or purpose.
8. Dissolving, liquidating, or winding up the business.
9. Amending this agreement or the Corporate Charter.

### 2.6 Emergency Reserve Protection
The emergency reserve (~10% of agreed enterprise valuation) is ring-fenced exclusively for enterprise continuity and **can never be used or pledged to fund a departing founder's buyout**.
